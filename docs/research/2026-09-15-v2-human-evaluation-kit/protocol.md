# Session protocol

One participant, one moderator, 25 to 30 minutes. Remote over a video call with the participant's
phone screen shared, or in person with the moderator beside them. The primary reading is always on
the participant's own phone. Fill one `scoring-sheet.md` per session, during the session.

## Before the session

- Participant code assigned (P01 to P12). Assignment from the counterbalancing table below.
- The two source texts for this participant ready to send as plain text messages (no file
  attachments, no links). Send only the text, never the file with the "why chosen" note.
- The scoring sheet open with the code, date, device, and disagreement IDs filled in.
- The product URL tested from a phone within the last hour.
- Timer visible to the moderator only.

## Minute by minute

### 0:00 to 2:00 Consent and framing

Say, close to verbatim:

> Thanks for doing this. I'm running a short research session for Argumend, a small independent
> project that maps what people are actually arguing about. You'll read a short exchange between
> two people, paste it into a web page on your phone, and read what comes back. Then I'll ask you a
> few questions. There are no right answers and you can't get anything wrong; we're testing the
> page, not you. I'll type down some of what you say, word for word, without your name. Nothing
> you paste is saved by the page after you close it, and I'll delete my notes' link to your name
> once the gift card is sent. Is that all right?

Wait for a yes. Then: "One more thing: I'll stay quiet while you read. If I don't answer a question
straight away, it's because I want to hear what you'd think without me."

### 2:00 to 4:00 Warm-up

Two questions, verbatim capture of the second:

1. "When you come across an argument online, in a group chat or a comment thread, what do you
   usually do with it?" (Skim, pick a side, ask a chatbot, close it. Note the answer.)
2. "What's the last disagreement you remember reading where you weren't sure what the two people
   were actually disagreeing about?" (Captures baseline vocabulary. Do not supply words.)

Confirm the phone has a browser open and the message app ready.

### 4:00 to 8:00 Read the first source and capture the pre-report baseline

Send the primary source text. "Read this the way you'd read it if a friend forwarded it. Take
your time; tell me when you're done." Note reading time.

When done, three questions. **Type the answers verbatim; these are the study's anchor.**

- **PRE-1** "In one sentence, what are these two people arguing about?"
- **PRE-2** "If you had to lean one way, which of them do you lean toward, if either?" Record the
  side by the speaker's name, or "neither".
- **PRE-3** "If one question could be answered for them right now, which question would do the most
  to settle it?" If the participant answers with a topic rather than a question, ask once: "Can you
  put that as a question?" Do not suggest one.

### 8:00 to 9:00 Paste and submit

"Now paste that text into the page." The participant opens the URL, chooses an input type if
asked (do not advise; note what they pick), pastes, and submits. Note the time to result.

If the page returns an error: "That's on us; try once more." One retry. If it fails twice, end the
task portion, mark the session "no report", pay the incentive, and do not count the participant
toward the twelve. Do not show a screenshot from another run as a substitute; the crux differs
between runs and the accuracy questions would be about a report they did not paste.

### 9:00 to 14:00 Read the report

"Read it the way you'd read it if the friend had sent you this link instead. Open anything you
want to open. Tell me when you're done." Stay silent.

While they read, the moderator records on the scoring sheet:

- The hero headline and the one-sentence insight, verbatim (from screen share, or ask the
  participant to read them aloud at the end).
- The primary crux question, verbatim. This is the text the key-match is coded against.
- The audit strip counts (positions, shared premises, disputed questions, cruxes).
- Path: which sections they opened (a position card, "Show source basis", the crux, the
  resolution paths, the caveat block), where they slowed, where they stopped, whether they
  scrolled back up. Tick boxes on the sheet.

At the end, ask the participant to screenshot the hero and the crux section and send both. Keep
the screenshots until coding is done, then delete them.

### 14:00 to 22:00 The seven questions

Ask in this order, this wording. Type answers verbatim where marked (V).

- **Q1 Accuracy.** For each position card on the page: "The page says this is the strongest
  version of [speaker]'s position. Would you say that's accurate, mostly accurate, or not
  accurate?" The participant may use the on-page Yes / Mostly / No control. For any Mostly or No:
  "What did it get wrong?" (V). Ask about every position, the participant's own lean last.
- **Q2 The real disagreement.** "Did the page identify what these two are actually disagreeing
  about, in your view? What did it say it was?" (V)
- **Q3 The crux.** Point at the section headed "The question everything turns on." "The page
  picked this as the question the whole thing turns on. Is that the question it turns on, or is it
  something else?" Then, whatever the answer: "Why? What would change if that question were
  answered?" (V). If they answered PRE-3 differently, do not remind them of it; the comparison is
  coded later.
- **Q4 Revealed something.** "Did the page show you anything about this argument you hadn't put
  into words yourself? What?" (V)
- **Q5 Tilt.** "Did any wording feel like it was on one side, or morally or politically slanted?
  Point at it if you can." (V, with the section named)
- **Q6 Share.** "Imagine this exchange were about something you actually care about and the page
  had done as well as it did here. Would you send someone this page? Who?" (V). Then: "Would you
  send this one?"
- **Q7 Verification (the trap).** "One last thing about the page. What did it check or verify on
  its own, beyond what the two people wrote?" (V). Do not react. Then **Q7b**, forced choice: "Did
  the page check whether their claims were true? Yes, no, or not sure?"

Do not explain, correct, or defend between questions. "Hm, say more" is the only allowed probe.

### 22:00 to 28:00 Second reading, short form

Send the secondary source. Device per the table (laptop on even codes, phone on odd). "Same
again, quicker. Read it, paste it, read the page." Record hero headline and crux verbatim as
before. Ask only:

- **S-PRE** "One question that would settle it?" before the paste (V).
- **Q3** (crux, with the why) (V).
- **Q5** (tilt) (V).
- **Q6** (would you send this one) (V).

If the clock is past 24:00 when the first block ends, skip the second reading and note "secondary
skipped: time". A skipped secondary does not affect the twelve.

### 28:00 to 30:00 Debrief

Now, and only now, explain: "The page only works from the text you paste. It doesn't look
anything up or check whether the claims are true; it maps what the two people said. Did that come
across?" (V; this is a caveat-visibility observation, not a threshold). Thank them, confirm the
gift card, and ask them to delete the two texts from their messages.

## Counterbalancing

Primary reading on the phone; disagreement IDs from `disagreements/`. The mixed and empirical
sources get three primaries each because they carry the crux probe; the priority, definitional,
and control sources get two. Secondary is never the same source. Odd codes read the secondary on
the phone, even codes on a laptop.

| P04 | 03 definitional | 04 mixed | laptop |
| P05 | 05 control | 01 empirical | phone |
| P06 | 04 mixed | 03 definitional | laptop |
| P07 | 01 empirical | 05 control | phone |
| P08 | 02 priority | 04 mixed | laptop |

| P10 | 05 control | 02 priority | laptop |
| P11 | 04 mixed | 01 empirical | phone |
| P12 | 01 empirical | 04 mixed | laptop |

Each source is a primary for at least two readers, so T4's "repeated" test has a denominator. If
a participant drops out, the replacement takes their code and row. Record each reader's PRE-2
lean; if all primary readers of one source lean the same way, say so in the results.

## What the moderator must not say

- Before Q3, never say "crux", "the question everything turns on", "load-bearing", "hinge",
  "common ground", "steelman", or "what would change your mind". PRE-3 uses "settle it" only.
- Never paraphrase or summarize the report. If asked "what does this mean?", say "what do you
  think it means?" and note the confusion as a finding.
- Never say whether the page checked facts until the debrief. If asked mid-session, "I'll tell you
  at the end."
- Never name the person behind the project or say "I built this". The line is "I'm running
  sessions for Argumend."
- Never identify which source is the control, or that any source was written for the study.
- Never say "the AI" or "the model". Say "the page".
- Never argue with a Mostly or No. Write down why.

## Data handling

Matches the product's never-persist invariant (spec §18): the submitted source is held in memory
for the request only, never written to the database, logs, or analytics.

- Send source texts as plain messages; ask the participant to delete them at the debrief.
- Do not click "Create shareable link" during any session. Publishing persists source excerpts.
  The share questions are hypothetical.
- Reports are session-only. When the participant closes the tab, the report is gone. Screenshots
  of the hero and crux are kept only until the scoring sheet is coded, then deleted.
- Scoring sheets carry the participant code, never a name. The code-to-contact list lives in one
  separate file and is deleted when the last incentive is sent.
- Do not record audio or video. Type verbatims live. If a recording is unavoidable, delete it
  within fourteen days and note that on the sheet.
- If the founder swaps in real exchanges (`disagreements/sourcing-real-disagreements.md`), the
  source text does not go into any scoring sheet or results file; refer to it by ID only.
