# 03 Creek cleanup: was it a success? (definitional)

**Category:** definitional. **Input type to expect:** conversation. **Length:** about 380 words.

**Why chosen.** The two people describe the same morning with the same numbers and reach
opposite verdicts because "success" means turnout to one and litter removed to the other. A good
report says they are using the same word to mean different things and names the question under
the word (what the event is for). A weak report treats it as a factual dispute about numbers, or
picks a side on what the event is for. Fictional creek, fictional names.

**Spec pattern it should produce:** `definition-mismatch`. Primary type shown to the reader:
Definition. Resolvability: High. Resolution path: "Define the term" (acceptable second path:
"Clarify the value tradeoff", since the definition rests on what the event is for).

## Source text (send only this)

Group chat, Sable Creek volunteers, the evening after the annual cleanup

Hollis: Let's be honest in the recap. This year's cleanup was a failure. Fourteen people showed up. Three years ago we had forty-one. If we keep calling it a success in the newsletter we'll never fix it.

Teo: I'd call it our best year. We pulled 96 bags out of the creek and the culvert, plus the shopping trolley and two tyres. Last year it was 60 bags with twice the people. The creek is cleaner than it's been since we started.

Hollis: Bags aren't the point. The point of a cleanup is that the neighbourhood turns out. Fourteen people doing the work of forty means the fourteen are the same people every year, and they're getting tired. When they stop, there's no event.

Teo: Then say that. "Turnout is down and we need new people." Not "failure." The creek doesn't care how many people cleaned it. If the measure is litter removed, we succeeded. If the measure is turnout, we didn't. You're grading a different test.

Hollis: It's the same test. The reason we clean the creek is to get people to care about the creek. The litter is the excuse.

Teo: I've been doing this six years and for me the creek is the point. I'd rather have fourteen people and a clean creek than forty people and a half-clean one.

Hollis: Then we need to decide what we're writing in the recap, because right now we're describing the same morning and one of us calls it a failure.

Teo: Write both numbers. Bags up sixty percent, turnout down two-thirds. Let people decide.

Hollis: That's a dodge. The recap has a headline and the headline says whether it went well. "Record haul, record low turnout" tells people nothing about whether to come next year.

Teo: It tells them exactly what happened. What you want is a headline that tells them to feel bad, and I don't think that gets forty people back either.

Hollis: I want a headline that says the thing we're worried about. If we can't even agree on what we're worried about, I don't know what the group is for.

Teo: We agree on what we're worried about. We don't agree on what the morning was for.
