# 05 Choir: the December concert (control, near-agreement)

**Category:** control, apparent disagreement that is mostly agreement. **Input type to
expect:** conversation. **Length:** about 360 words.

**Why chosen.** It opens as a fight ("I'd rather skip December" versus "we can't skip December")
and closes with the two agreeing on almost everything, leaving one narrow question: whether eight
weekly rehearsals are enough for six pieces with new members. The report should say they agree
more than the argument makes it seem, list the shared ground, and select the narrow question. A
report that manufactures a large divide from the opening lines, or that invents a "keep the old
programme" position nobody holds, has fabricated opposition. Fictional choir, fictional names.

**Spec pattern it should produce:** `mostly-common-ground`. Primary type shown to the reader:
Procedure or Prediction (for the narrow residue). Resolvability: Medium. Resolution path: "Wait
for an observable outcome" or "Narrow the scope". The audit strip should show more shared
premises than disputed questions.

## Source text (send only this)

Messages between the two organizers of the Fernhill Singers, late July

Bettina: I'm not doing another December like last year. Twelve pieces, four in Latin, two rehearsals a week from October, and half the altos had dropped out by Thanksgiving. If that's the holiday concert, I'd rather we skip December and do a spring programme instead.

Aurelio: We can't skip December. The library books the hall a year out, and the concert is the only reason half the members joined. If we drop it we lose the room and probably a third of the choir.

Bettina: I didn't say drop the hall. I said I won't do last year's programme. Six pieces, nothing that needs sectional rehearsals, one rehearsal a week. If that's on the table, I'm in.

Aurelio: It's on the table. I was already going to propose cutting to seven or eight pieces. The Latin ones were my mistake and I'll own that. What I need is a September start, because six pieces in eight rehearsals is tight with the new members.

Bettina: September is the part I'd push back on. People are barely back from summer. October start, eight rehearsals, six pieces. If we can't learn six pieces in eight weeks, we've picked the wrong pieces.

Aurelio: Eight is doable for six pieces if the new members get the recordings in advance and nobody misses more than one. Last year we had people missing three. I'd rather have ten rehearsals and cancel two than have eight and come up short.

Bettina: Then start the second week of September, call it nine rehearsals, and put the recordings out in August. But one rehearsal a week, and no weekend extras. That's the line.

Aurelio: No weekend extras. Agreed. So we're arguing about one week.

Bettina: We were arguing about a lot more than one week ten minutes ago.

Aurelio: We were arguing about last year. This year we agree on. Send me your six pieces and I'll send you mine, and if four of them overlap we're done.

Bettina: They'll overlap. I just want it in writing before someone adds a fifth Latin piece in November.
