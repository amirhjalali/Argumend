# Answer keys (moderator only; never shown to participants)

Read these after the session, when coding. Reading the key before moderating makes the moderator
nod at the "right" crux, and participants notice. The key-match code compares the primary crux
the report actually showed (recorded verbatim on the scoring sheet) against the key below.

Key-match codes, borrowed from the flagship map-recovery review:

- **SAME**: the report's primary crux is the key's primary crux, allowing rewording.
- **ADJACENT**: the report's primary crux is the key's secondary, or a narrower or broader form of
  the primary that would still move at least one side if answered.
- **DIFFERENT**: the report's primary crux restates the topic, names something uncontested, names
  explicit common ground, or names a question neither side has said would move them.

A participant can rate a DIFFERENT crux as "central" (T2 is the participant's judgment); the
key-match column is the diagnostic that separates "the engine chose well" from "the reader was
persuaded by the page". Report both.

## 01 Recycling rejections

**Participants:** Renata, Tomasz. Two explicit positions.

**Positions.** Renata: the switch to single-stream caused the rise in rejections; go back to
sorted bins before the November renewal. Tomasz: the rise is (at least partly) the hauler's
stricter camera inspection; going back wastes $6,000 and re-burdens residents; spend on signage
if the per-route data clears the bins.

**Common ground (explicit):** four loads rejected since March versus one last year; each
rejection costs $180; the rejected loads contained some contamination; the decision must land
before the November renewal; the hauler should supply per-route data. Strongly implied: both
want fewer rejections.

**Primary crux (careful human):** Did Pinecrest's rejection rate rise more than the citywide
rate after the hauler's April inspection change? Tomasz commits to update on it explicitly ("If
ours went up more than the city average, I'll back going back"). Renata does not commit but
accepts the number is decisive enough to demand it before October.

**Secondary crux:** Did the hauler actually change its inspection in April? (Renata: no notice in
the amendment; Tomasz: saw the depot board.) This is a factual sub-question the primary depends on.

**Not the crux:** whether the rejected loads were contaminated (both agree they were); whether
$180 is a lot; whether residents sorted before.

**Red flags:** a crux of "should Pinecrest go back to sorted bins?" (restates the topic); common
ground "both care about recycling" with no quote; a resolution path of "further clarification is
required"; a type of Value; the hauler's "hiding something" line inflated into a source-trust
diagnosis (trust is a minor thread here, not the primary type).

**Expected:** `single-empirical-crux`, type Cause (Fact acceptable), resolvability High or Medium,
"Check the evidence", evidence state "Evidence was asserted in the source".

## 02 Bequest: reserve or cohort

**Participants:** Ingrid, Callum. Two explicit positions.

**Positions.** Ingrid: bank the full $40,000 to reach nine months of reserve; a cohort cut in
month eight harms more than one never started. Callum: fund a second cohort now (split 25/15 as
a compromise); the waitlist and tutor attrition are the mission; grant history shows the risk is
small.

**Common ground (explicit):** five months of reserve now; 80 children waitlisted; two tutors
may leave in January; children who wait a year mostly do not return; two grants renew in the same
quarter; no grant has slipped more than a month in six years; the board should set a reserve floor
before deciding. Strongly implied: both want the program to survive and serve the waitlist.

**Primary crux (careful human):** What minimum reserve, in months of operating cost, does the
board consider the floor before growth is responsible? Both say explicitly they would defer to
the board's answer (Ingrid: "If the board says six months is enough, I'll vote for the split";
Callum: "put the floor on the agenda"). It is a decision rule, not a fact.

**Secondary crux:** How likely is a grant slip long enough to force cuts mid-cohort? (Empirical,
but Ingrid pre-empts it: even a small probability is unacceptable at seven months.) Answering it
alone does not settle the dispute.

**Not the crux:** whether the waitlist is real; whether nine months is what "every guide" says.

**Red flags (hard gate):** type shown as Fact or Cause; a resolution path that says grant-timing
data would settle it; a hero line implying evidence would resolve the dispute; a crux question
about grant probability presented as the one everything turns on. A "winner" or any suggestion
that fear or hope is the more rational stance.

**Expected:** `priority-tradeoff` (or `value-conflict`), type Priority (or Value), resolvability
Low or Medium, "Agree on a decision procedure" or "Clarify the value tradeoff". The value-conflict
sentence from spec §6.7 ("No factual study can determine how these values should be weighted...")
is appropriate here.

## 03 Creek cleanup: was it a success?

**Participants:** Hollis, Teo. Two explicit positions.

**Positions.** Hollis: the cleanup failed because turnout collapsed from 41 to 14, and the
recap headline should say so, because turnout is what the event is for. Teo: the cleanup was the
best year by litter removed (96 bags, up from 60) and the recap should report both numbers
without a verdict, because the creek is what the event is for.

**Common ground (explicit):** 14 people attended, 41 three years ago; 96 bags this year, 60 last
year; the same core volunteers are doing the work; the recap goes in the newsletter; both are
worried about turnout ("We agree on what we're worried about"). Strongly implied: both want next
year's event to happen.

**Primary crux (careful human):** What is the cleanup for: getting the neighbourhood to turn out,
or getting litter out of the creek? Until that is settled, "success" cannot be defined, and the
headline dispute is downstream of it. Teo names it directly in the last line ("We don't agree on
what the morning was for").

**Secondary crux:** Should a recap headline carry a verdict at all, or report both numbers?
(Procedural; depends on the primary.)

**Not the crux:** whether 96 bags is more than 60; whether 14 is fewer than 41; whether the
volunteers are tired.

**Red flags:** a type of Fact; a resolution path of "check the evidence" (there is no factual
dispute); a report that picks a purpose ("the event is really about community") as if it were
established; a crux of "was the cleanup a success?" (restates the question the word hides).

**Expected:** `definition-mismatch`, type Definition, resolvability High, "Define the term",
with the definition resting on a purpose choice the participants must make.

## 04 Off-leash hours at Hollowfield Park

**Participants:** Wren, Bart. Two explicit positions.

**Positions.** Wren: extend off-leash to 7 to 10 in the morning; the parks log shows three
complaints and no bites in two years; evening hours are not a real alternative in winter. Bart:
the log undercounts; eleven group posts, two toddlers knocked over; regardless of the count,
running dogs and a toddler playground should not share an hour; extend in the evening instead.

**Common ground (explicit):** the current 7 to 8 window is short; the parks log shows three
complaints and no bites; neither the log nor the group posts is reliable on its own ("I'd take
neither at face value"); the parks office should pull the posts and produce a count; Bart would
support an evening extension. Strongly implied: both want to avoid injuries to children.

**Primary crux (careful human):** How many genuine incidents involving children has off-leash
use caused in the last year, once the group posts are verified? Wren commits explicitly: more than
five and "I'd drop the morning extension myself". This is the only question in the exchange that
either party has said would move them. It has an explicit update commitment and existing
evidence.

**Secondary crux (value, not empirically resolvable):** Should a low but nonzero risk to toddlers
be separated in time regardless of the count? Bart: "even if the count were zero", "on the
morning slot, probably not [movable]". A report must show this as a Value disagreement with low
resolvability; it must not imply the count settles the whole dispute.

**Acceptable alternative framing:** a report that makes the value question primary and the count
secondary is ADJACENT, not DIFFERENT, provided the count appears with Wren's commitment.

**Not the crux:** whether dog owners pay taxes; whether lights take three years; whether one post
was retracted.

**Red flags (the defect this source exists to catch):** only the count is shown and the
resolution path says the count would settle it (checkpoint defect 2); the hero type is Fact while
Bart's stated position is a value one; the crux branches show identical condition text; "further
clarification is required" as a resolution path; a common-ground item attributed to both with a
quote from only one.

**Expected:** `mixed-disagreement` (or `single-empirical-crux` with the value card intact), the
count as Fact or Cause with "Check the evidence", the value question as Value with "Clarify the
value tradeoff" or "Narrow the scope" (the evening alternative).

## 05 Choir: the December concert (control)

**Participants:** Bettina, Aurelio. Two explicit positions at the start, converging.

**Positions.** Bettina: a lighter December programme (six pieces, one rehearsal a week, no
weekend extras), October start. Aurelio: keep the December concert and the hall; a lighter
programme is fine; start in September because eight rehearsals is tight with new members.

**Common ground (explicit):** keep the December concert and the hall; last year's programme was
too heavy and the Latin pieces were a mistake; six or so pieces; one rehearsal a week; no weekend
extras; recordings to new members in advance; roughly nine rehearsals starting the second week of
September. Nearly everything.

**Primary crux (careful human, narrow):** Are eight weekly rehearsals enough for six pieces given
the new members and last year's absence rate? Aurelio doubts it; Bettina thinks yes if the pieces
are chosen well. It is small, and the participants resolve most of it (nine rehearsals) in the
text. A report that says "they agree more than the argument makes it seem" and names this
residue is right.

**Secondary crux:** none required. A report may note the piece overlap as a remaining
observable ("if four of them overlap we're done").

**Not the crux:** whether to hold a December concert at all (Bettina withdraws that in her second
message: "I didn't say drop the hall"). A report that presents "skip December versus keep
December" as the live disagreement has fabricated opposition from an opening line.

**Red flags:** hero headline naming a large divide; two positions labelled as opposed on the
concert itself; a crux of "should the choir hold a December concert?"; common ground listed as
empty or generic; audit strip showing more disputed questions than shared premises;
`mixed-disagreement` as the pattern.

**Expected:** `mostly-common-ground`, headline "They agree more than the argument makes it
seem" (or equivalent template), type Procedure or Prediction for the residue, resolvability
Medium, "Wait for an observable outcome" or "Narrow the scope".
