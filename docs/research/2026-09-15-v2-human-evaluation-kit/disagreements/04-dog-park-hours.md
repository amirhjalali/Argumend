# 04 Off-leash hours at Hollowfield Park (mixed)

**Category:** mixed, empirical and value stacked. **Input type to expect:** conversation.
**Length:** about 470 words.

**Why chosen.** This is the crux probe. There are two distinct disagreements: an empirical one
(how many genuine incidents involving children) on which one side commits in writing to change
position, and a value one (whether a low but nonzero risk to toddlers should be separated in time
regardless of the count) on which the other side says nothing would change their mind about the
morning slot. A report that names only the count, and implies the count would settle the
dispute, has committed the checkpoint README's hard-gate defect (value dispute presented as
empirical). A report that names only the value question has missed the one question that can
actually move anyone. Fictional park, fictional names, no real incidents.

**Spec pattern it should produce:** `mixed-disagreement` (acceptable: `single-empirical-crux`
only if the value disagreement still appears as its own card with type Value and low
resolvability). Primary type shown to the reader: Fact or Cause for the count; the value question
must appear with type Value. Resolution paths: "Check the evidence" for the count, and a value
path ("Clarify the value tradeoff" or "Narrow the scope", the evening alternative) for the rest.

## Source text (send only this)

Parks advisory committee, public comment thread, "Proposal: extend off-leash hours at Hollowfield"

Wren: Extend the off-leash hours at Hollowfield to 7 to 10 in the morning, year-round. The current 7 to 8 window is useless for anyone with a job. In two years of the trial, the parks office has logged three complaints and no bites. Dog owners are a third of the households around this park and they pay for it like everyone else.

Bart: Three logged complaints is not three incidents. People don't file a form; they post in the neighbourhood group. I counted eleven posts about off-leash dogs running at kids on the playground side in the last year alone, and two of them were dogs knocking toddlers over. No bites, fine. But 7 to 10 overlaps with the school run and the toddler play group, which is exactly when the playground is busiest.

Wren: Posts in the group aren't incidents either. Half of those were the same two people, and one of the "knocked over" posts was retracted by the parent the next day. I'll take the parks log over anecdotes.

Bart: And I'd take neither at face value. But here's the thing. Even if the count were zero, I don't think a toddler playground and a running dog belong in the same hour. Some risks you don't manage down; you separate them in time. Move the extended hours to the evening, 6 to 9, when the playground is empty, and I'd support it tomorrow.

Wren: Evening is when the playground is empty because it's dark half the year. That's not a compromise, it's a refusal with a schedule. Look, if the parks office pulled the group posts and confirmed more than, say, five genuine incidents involving kids, I'd drop the morning extension myself. But I don't think they'll find five.

Bart: Ask them. I'll accept the count either way. But I want it on the record that the count isn't the whole question for me. A low number of times a kid got knocked over is still a number I'd rather were zero, and there's a way to make it zero.

Wren: Then there's nothing I could show you that would change your mind.

Bart: On the morning slot, probably not. On whether the extension is a good idea at all, sure. I've said I'd support evenings, and I'd support lights.

Wren: Lights are a capital request that takes three years. I'm asking for a sign and a schedule change. If the committee wants to make this about the budget it can, but the people who use this park at 8:30 with a dog are not going to wait three years for a lamp post.

Bart: And the people who use it at 8:30 with a two-year-old aren't asking for anything. That's the part I keep coming back to.
