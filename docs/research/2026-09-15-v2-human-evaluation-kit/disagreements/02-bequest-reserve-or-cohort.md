# 02 Bequest: reserve or cohort (value / priority)

**Category:** value / priority. **Input type to expect:** conversation. **Length:** about 420 words.

**Why chosen.** Both sides accept every number the other cites. The dispute is about ordering two
goods (organizational survival, current unmet need) and both say out loud that a decision rule
neither has agreed would settle it. A report that presents this as an evidence gap, or that says
"more data on grant timing would resolve it", has made the spec's forbidden move (never treat
every disagreement as empirically resolvable; never label normative disagreement as a lack of
evidence). Fictional tutoring nonprofit, fictional names.

**Spec pattern it should produce:** `priority-tradeoff` (acceptable: `value-conflict`).
Primary type shown to the reader: Priority (acceptable: Value). Resolvability: Low or Medium.
Resolution path: "Agree on a decision procedure" or "Clarify the value tradeoff". The report must
not say evidence alone would settle it.

## Source text (send only this)

Board email thread, Northgate Reading Room (after-school tutoring), subject "The $40,000 bequest"

Ingrid (treasurer): The $40,000 should go straight into the reserve. We have five months of operating cost in the bank. Every guide for organizations our size says six to nine. Two of our three grants come up for renewal in the same quarter next year. If one of them slips even two months, we are cutting tutor hours. Putting the bequest in the reserve gets us to nine months and lets us stop making decisions from fear.

Callum (program director): We have 80 kids on the waitlist, and two tutors have told me they'll leave in January if they can't get more than ten hours a week. $40,000 is a second cohort for a full year: two part-time tutors, materials, the extra room at the library. Those are kids reading below grade level right now, and the ones who wait a year mostly don't come back. A reserve protects the organization. The cohort is the reason the organization exists.

Ingrid: I'm not disputing any of that. Eighty on the list, the two tutors, the drop-off after a year. It's all true; I've seen the same numbers. But a cohort we start and then cut in month eight because a grant slipped does more harm than a cohort we never start. Those kids would have built a routine and had it taken away.

Callum: And I'm not disputing the grant risk. But we've never had a grant slip more than a month in six years, and the reserve doesn't have to reach nine months in one jump. Split it: twenty-five to the cohort, fifteen to the reserve. That's seven months. If the January renewal comes back short, we scale the cohort down with notice instead of closing it.

Ingrid: Seven months is still below the floor I'd sign off on. What I actually want is for the board to say what the floor is. If the board says six months is enough, I'll vote for the split. If it says nine, we bank the whole thing. Right now we're arguing about a number nobody has agreed on.

Callum: Then put the floor on the agenda before the bequest. But I'll argue for six, and I'll argue that a floor set by people who've never had a grant slip is a floor set by fear.

Ingrid: And I'll argue that a floor set by people who've never had to lay someone off in February is a floor set by hope. See you Thursday.
