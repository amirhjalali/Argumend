# 01 Recycling rejections (empirical)

**Category:** empirical. **Input type to expect:** conversation. **Length:** about 390 words.

**Why chosen.** Both sides accept the same four events and the same money at stake. The dispute
is about what caused a number to move, one side names a checkable fact that would change their
position, and there is a deadline. This is the cleanest test of whether the report finds the one
question that would move the dispute rather than restating the topic. No identity, health,
politics, or real people; the residents' association and hauler are fictional.

**Spec pattern it should produce:** `single-empirical-crux` (acceptable: `causal-model-split`).
Primary type shown to the reader: Cause (acceptable: Fact). Resolvability: High or Medium.
Resolution path: "Check the evidence". Evidence state: "Evidence was asserted in the source".

## Source text (send only this)

Pinecrest Commons residents' forum, thread "Recycling: go back to sorted bins before November?"

Renata: Since we switched to single-stream bins in March, the hauler has rejected four of our loads as contaminated. In the whole of last year they rejected one. Each rejection costs the association $180 and the load goes to landfill anyway. The switch made contamination worse, and we should go back to sorted bins before the contract renews in November.

Tomasz: The rejection count is real, but it doesn't show the bins are the cause. In April the hauler put camera inspection on the truck arms. I saw the notice on the depot board. Every route in the city started seeing more rejections after that. Our contamination may be exactly what it was; the inspection got stricter. Going back to sorted bins throws away the $6,000 we spent on the new bins and puts sorting back on residents who were skipping it entirely before.

Renata: If the inspection changed, the hauler would have told us in the contract notice. I read the March amendment and there is nothing about cameras. And even if they did get stricter, contamination that gets caught is still contamination. The four rejected loads had plastic bags and food waste in them, according to the tags.

Tomasz: The tags say "plastic film / organics," which is what every rejection tag in the city says. I'm not claiming the loads were clean. I'm saying you can't compare four rejections under a camera regime to one rejection under a guy-glances-in-the-truck regime. Ask the hauler for their rejection rate per route, before and after April. If ours went up more than the city average, I'll back going back to sorted bins. If ours tracks the average, the bins aren't the problem and we should spend on signage instead.

Renata: Fine. That's a number the hauler should have. But I want it before the October meeting, because if we miss the November renewal we're locked into single-stream for three more years, and I'm not paying $180 a month to find out you were right.

Tomasz: Agreed on the deadline. I'll email the account manager tonight and copy the board. If they won't give us the per-route numbers, that tells us something too.

Renata: It tells us they're hiding something. It doesn't tell us the bins are fine.
