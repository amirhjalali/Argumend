# Recruiting

Target fourteen confirmed: two for the pilot, twelve for the study. Expect two no-shows; recruit
sixteen if the channel allows.

## Who to recruit

The go/no-go question in the north star names the reader: "a skeptical reader" who spends five
minutes on a page. Recruit ordinary adults who read arguments online, not debate coaches and not
fans of the project. The baseline in `docs/research/2026-08-10-north-star-baseline.md` came from
"a handful of people" the founder sent a link to, and the result was muted and polite. Friends of
the moderator give muted answers. Prefer friends-of-friends and strangers from a community post.

Mix of views. The five sources are local disputes rather than national politics, so the relevant
mix is in temperament and stake, not party. Aim for roughly half on each of these splits:

- Risk-averse versus growth-minded (the priority source).
- Dog owner versus parent of a small child, or neither (the mixed source).
- Regular volunteer or organizer versus not (the definitional and control sources).
- Picks a side quickly versus slowly (screener item 6).

## Screener

Send as a short form or ask in the reply thread. Six items plus two exclusions.

1. Age 18 or over? (must be yes)
2. Do you have a smartphone with a web browser you can share on a video call, or can you meet in
   person? (must be yes to one)
3. In a typical week, how often do you read an argument between people online (comments, group
   chats, forums, threads)? Never / a few times a month / most days.
   (Exclude "never".)
4. Do you own a dog? Do you have a child under eight at home? Do you volunteer or organize for a
   community group? Have you ever sat on a board or handled a budget for a club or nonprofit?
   (Used only to balance; no answer excludes.)
5. Have you heard of a website called Argumend, or seen any argument-mapping tool in the last
   year? (Exclude anyone who has visited argumend.org or knows what it does.)
6. When you read two people arguing, do you usually decide who's right within a minute, or do you
   tend to stay undecided for a while? (Balance.)
7. Do you know the person who will be running the session socially? (Exclude close friends and
   family; acquaintances are fine, note it on the sheet.)
8. Do you work on AI products, argument or debate tools, or content moderation? (Exclude; their
   feedback is useful but belongs in a different study.)

## Recruiting messages

Both in the institutional voice. No founder name, no "I built this", no claims about what the
product does beyond one plain sentence.

### Community post (a local forum, a neighbourhood group, a hobby Discord, a subreddit that allows research posts)

> **30-minute paid research session: read an argument, tell us what it was about**
>
> Argumend is a small independent project that maps what people are actually arguing about. We're
> looking for a dozen people to spend 30 minutes on a video call: you'll read a short exchange
> between two people, paste it into a web page on your phone, read what comes back, and answer a
> few questions. No preparation, no expertise needed, and there are no wrong answers.
>
> You'll get a 30 [USD or local equivalent] gift card. We don't record video or audio and we
> don't keep what you paste. Sessions are this week and next, evenings and weekends available.
>
> If you're interested, reply or message with two or three times that suit you. Please don't look
> the site up beforehand; it matters that you see it fresh.

### Direct ask (an acquaintance, or a friend passing it to their acquaintance)

> Hi [name]. Would you have 30 minutes this week or next for a paid research session? It's for
> Argumend, a small independent project that maps what people are actually arguing about. You'd
> read a short exchange between two people, paste it into a web page on your phone, read what
> comes back, and answer some questions. 30 [USD] gift card, no prep, no recording, nothing you
> paste is kept. If yes, send me two or three times that work and whether a video call or meeting
> in person is easier. One ask: don't look the site up first.

If a friend is forwarding it: "Feel free to pass this to someone who'd be honest about it. We'd
rather hear from people who don't know us."

## Incentive

30 USD gift card (or local equivalent) for a 30-minute session, sent the same day. Pilot
participants get the same. A "no report" session (the page failed twice) still pays. Do not offer
early access, credit, or a link to the product as the incentive; it selects for fans.

## Scheduling template

Offer slots in the recruiting reply. Confirmation message:

> Confirmed: [day, date, time, time zone], about 30 minutes, [video link / address].
>
> What you'll need: your phone with a browser, and if we're on a video call, the ability to share
> your phone screen (on most phones: join the call from the phone, then tap share screen; I'll talk
> you through it). If screen sharing isn't possible, that's fine, you can read a couple of things
> aloud instead.
>
> There's nothing to prepare, and please don't look up the site beforehand. I'll send the gift
> card right after the session. Reply "yes" to confirm, or send another time if this one no longer
> works.

Reminder the morning of: "See you at [time] today for the 30-minute Argumend session. Phone with
a browser is all you need." Assign the participant code when the confirmation arrives and enter it
in the counterbalancing table in `protocol.md`.
