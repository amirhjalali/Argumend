# Argumend product critique

## 1. First-screen verdict (30 words)

Bounce. Phone opens to a library card: inventory counts, four thesis paragraphs, zero hook. No punchline, no “wait—what?”, no reason this isn’t a PDF dump. Scroll is work, not curiosity.

## 2. Top 10 problems (by share impact)

**1. Opening is inventory, not insight**  
Offending: `4 positions · 54 claims · 50 pieces of evidence · 31 open disputes · 5 cruxes` under a dry title.  
Why it fails: Stats signal database completeness, not a reason to care. Shareable things open with the surprising *shape* of the fight, not a contents list.  
Fix: Lead with one concrete, stolen sentence. e.g. *“The data says early-career AI jobs fell ~16%. The fight is whether that’s AI—or rates, offshoring, and a definition of ‘unemployment’ nobody agrees on.”* Hide the counts below the fold or in a quiet footer.

**2. Position cards are steel-man essays, not positions**  
Offending: Displacement-now — *“AI is already causing measurable, AI-caused employment displacement concentrated in early-career and AI-exposed white-collar work, and this will compound into a broad U.S. unemployment crisis within 15 years.”* Plus the other three at the same density.  
Why it fails: On mobile this is four walls of policy-paper prose. Nobody screenshots a paragraph that needs a second coffee.  
Fix: Name + one vivid line + one “who says this.” e.g. **Displacement-now** — *Junior white-collar is already bleeding; 15 years compounds it into crisis.* Held by: Stanford DEL, Census, frontier-lab voices. Put the full steel-man behind expand.

**3. The actual product—the cruxes—arrives too late and too long**  
Offending: Section title *“The debate mostly comes down to”* then crux 1 as a 40-word abstract: *“Firms facing improved AI capability respond primarily by reducing hiring and headcount growth rather than by expanding output or redesigning jobs…”*  
Why it fails: This *is* the five-minute promise. Buried under four position essays, written like a methods section. “Unresolved questions with the most leverage — computed from the argument structure, not editorial choice” is trust theater that kills heat.  
Fix: Put cruxes first (or immediately after a one-line position strip). Title each as a human bet: *“When AI gets better, do firms fire—or grow?”* Subhead the academic wording. Drop “computed / not editorial” until the footer.

**4. No emotional or identity hook for the person most likely to share**  
Offending: Nowhere on first screens: *you, your kids, your major, your first job.* Closest buried treasure: *Workers aged 22-25 in the most AI-exposed occupations experienced a 16% relative employment decline…* and *Women are exposed… roughly two to three times the rate of men…*  
Why it fails: Abstract macro fights don’t get forwarded. “This is about people like me / my cohort” does.  
Fix: Surface one identity-stakes line above the fold: *If you’re 22–25 in software, CS, or clerical work, this map is about your pipeline—not a future robot apocalypse.*

**5. “Explore the full argument” is a second database after the first**  
Offending: Endless expandable claims, each with *Status basis This is the polluter-pays position in the corpus's own framing…*  
Why it fails: After cruxes, the page becomes a corpus browser. Share is killed by the feeling “I’d need a weekend.” Payoff never peaks—it just continues.  
Fix: Cap the public path at ~5 cruxes + 3 “most surprising claims.” Put the rest behind “Show full map / researcher mode.” Default experience = argument, not archive.

**6. Voice is beige institutional: corpus, steel-man, balance, weight**  
Offending (everywhere): *“Well-mapped, genuinely contested”*; *“Balance 47 of 100, weight 69 of 100”*; homepage *“Map Arguments, Not Win Them”*; *“assembled by AI from a research corpus…”*  
Why it fails: Sounds like a compliance dashboard or a rationalist wiki. Zero edge. Nothing quotable. Nothing that hits a nerve.  
Fix: Kill balance/weight on consumer surfaces. Tag topics with human labels: *Hot fight · Quietly settling · Definition war.* Tagline candidates with teeth, not process: *“See what the fight is actually about.”* / *“Stop arguing past each other.”*

**7. Homepage and Explore don’t sell the flagship—they sell a catalog**  
Offending: Homepage hero *“Argument maps for difficult questions / See both sides of any controversial topic, mapped”* + trending rows of Balance/Weight; Explore featured is better but still *“5 computed cruxes · every claim, source, and assumption inspectable.”* Featured analysis is Consciousness in AI, not the mass-unemployment flagship.  
Why it fails: Catalog pages without a showpiece feel like empty stores. “Both sides” is 2016. Share starts from one unforgettable page, not 156.  
Fix: Homepage = one brutal flagship teaser (the AI jobs map) above the fold. Explore featured must match the product’s best work. “150+ topics” is proof later, not the pitch.

**8. Trust signals read as evasion, not confidence**  
Offending: Repeated *Interest note:*, *Scope note:*, *Unverified:*, *the corpus's own adversarial balance review*; methodology *“liveness-checked, adversarially reviewed for balance in five directions.”*  
Why it fails: Over-disclaiming before you’ve earned attention feels like a product ashamed of its own spine. Shareable products feel sure; they disclose once, cleanly.  
Fix: One short trust strip: sources linked, interests flagged, AI-assembled. Move the rest to About. Don’t put “thin evidence / hidden assumption” as first visual noise on every crux—put them as secondary chips after the human title.

**9. Definitional gold is buried as crux #4 bureaucracy**  
Offending: Crux 4 — *“'Mass unemployment' should be defined as sustained U-3 unemployment above 10% for multiple quarters, rather than by wage collapse…”*  
Why it fails: This is the buried lede of the whole page: **people are yelling past each other because they don’t mean the same disaster.** Presented as item 4 in a list of empirical mechanisms, it dies.  
Fix: Make it the frame of the page: *This question is three fights in a trench coat—jobs, wages, and power.* Then branch.

**10. Nothing is designed to be sent**  
Offending: No one-line “share card” claim; no “send this crux”; no stark contrast pair (e.g. Klarna “700 agents” vs. “never stopped outsourcing / later rehiring humans”); no ending that lands.  
Why it fails: Shareability is a product surface. Right now the sharpest facts are footnotes inside expandables.  
Fix: After cruxes, a single **“What you can honestly say after five minutes”** block with 3 bullets + a share button that packages *one* crux or *one* fact with the question.

## 3. The shareability question

For someone to text “look at this,” the page needs:

1. **A first screen that delivers a non-obvious structure** of the fight in under 10 seconds.  
2. **One stealable line** that upgrades the friend’s mental model.  
3. **A feeling of competence transfer** (“now I can argue smarter”) without homework vibes.  
4. **A clear peak**—then stop. Don’t keep opening cabinets.

**Most shareable thing buried right now**  
The middle position + definitional trap, packed with concrete teeth:

- Early-career AI-exposed workers: ~**16% relative employment decline** (Stanford) / **12%** (Census)—while **U-3 is still ~4%**.  
- “Adjustment” may mean sliding into **home-health jobs at ~$34.9k** (BLS largest growth occupation).  
- So the real fight often isn’t *mass unemployment*—it’s **wage collapse, career-ladder collapse, and who pays**, while headlines stay calm.

That’s the nerve. The page currently stores it like a filing cabinet.

Runner-up share-bomb (also buried): **Klarna’s “700 full-time agents”** story with the interest/scope notes that the equivalence ≠ layoffs and the firm later walked the AI-first posture back. That’s a perfect “everyone shared the wrong takeaway” card—and it’s nested under crux 1 evidence.

## 4. Three copy rewrites

**Worst #1**  
Quote: *“4 positions · 54 claims · 50 pieces of evidence · 31 open disputes · 5 cruxes”*  
Rewrite: *Four live camps. Five questions the whole fight actually turns on.*

**Worst #2**  
Quote: *“Unresolved questions with the most leverage — computed from the argument structure, not editorial choice.”*  
Rewrite: *These five disagreements decide almost everything else. Settle one of them and whole positions move.*

**Worst #3**  
Quote: *“Firms facing improved AI capability respond primarily by reducing hiring and headcount growth rather than by expanding output or redesigning jobs to use the freed-up capacity.”*  
Rewrite: *When AI makes workers more productive, do companies grow—or stop hiring?*  
(Keep the long form as “why this is the hinge.”)

**Honorable worst (homepage)**  
Quote: *“Map Arguments, Not Win Them”* / *“See both sides of any controversial topic, mapped”*  
Rewrite: *Disagree better. Start by seeing what the fight is actually about.*  
Or sharper: *Chatbots summarize sides. This shows you the hinges.*

## 5. What to cut (or hide)

**Cut from default consumer path**

- Balance X of 100 / Weight Y of 100 / “Well-mapped, genuinely contested” spam on homepage, trending, Explore cards.  
- Opening inventory counts (or demote hard).  
- Full multi-sentence “Held by:” blocks on first view (chip + expand).  
- “Status basis…” prose on every claim in the main scroll—researcher mode only.  
- Methodology parade on the topic page (*liveness-checked… five directions… reproducible*)—one line + link.  
- Duplicate claim sprawl after cruxes; ~45 “explore the full argument” items as default UX.  
- Homepage “Trending This Week” as a metric graveyard; either humanize or kill.  
- “JavaScript is required for the interactive argument maps” as a vibe-setter (if it appears before value).  
- Soft process branding: *steel-manned*, *corpus*, *computed cruxes* as hero language.

**Hide, don’t delete**

- Full claim graph, interest notes density, adversarial-balance story, source-interest essays.  
- Filters like evidence-balance sliders on Explore for first-time browsers—power users only.

**Structural cut**

- The path **question → four long positions → cruxes → infinite claims**.  
  Replace with **question → what the fight is really about (1 screen) → 5 cruxes → optional depth**.

---

**Bottom line:** The research spine has edges (cohort damage without macro crisis; definition games; Klarna mythology; who decides vs. who measures). The experience sandblasts them into beige corpus UI. Right now nothing hits a nerve because the product leads with *how thorough it is* instead of *what it changes in your head in five minutes*. Fix hierarchy and voice first; the share moment is already in the map—it’s just buried under the filing system.
