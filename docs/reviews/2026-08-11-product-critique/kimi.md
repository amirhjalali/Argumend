• # Argumend critique — insight-density lens

  ## 1. First-screen verdict

  They bounce. The first screen is a taxonomy — "4 positions · 54 claims · 50 pieces of evidence" — metadata about an argument, not an insight from it. Nothing on screen teaches anything yet. (28 words)

  ## 2. Top 10 problems, ranked by impact on shareability

  **1. The page opens with inventory, not insight.**
  > "4 positions · 54 claims · 50 pieces of evidence · 31 open disputes · 5 cruxes"

  This is a database schema announcing itself. A reader from a shared link wants to *learn something about AI and jobs*; instead they learn the page is large. "54 claims" is a workload, not a promise — it reads as "this will take an hour." Fix: replace the stat line with the single sharpest thing the map discovered, e.g. "The strongest evidence on each side: 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs are down 16% — but half the tech hiring collapse happened *before* ChatGPT existed." Numbers-as-content, not numbers-as-apparatus.

  **2. The positions are written for a seminar, not a phone.**
  > "AI is already causing measurable, AI-caused employment displacement concentrated in early-career and AI-exposed white-collar work, and this will compound into a broad U.S. unemployment crisis within 15 years."

  One sentence, 36 words, nominalized ("displacement," "concentrated," "compound"), with a redundancy ("measurable, AI-caused employment displacement"). All four position cards are like this — roughly 130 words of dense abstract prose before a single fact appears. First-screen learning yield: near zero. Fix: one-line plain-English stake per position ("AI is already eating entry-level jobs" / "We've panicked about machines for 250 years and always been wrong"), with the academic version behind the expand.

  **3. The killer facts are buried ~200 lines deep in the least visible section.**
  The most visceral, shareable content on the page — Kenyan labelers paid $1.32–$2/hour to review abuse content while OpenAI paid Sama $12.50/worker-hour; Black and non-degree workers 67% more likely to be displaced with earnings still down 25% a *decade* later; women exposed to AI at 2.7× men's rate; displaced clerks rehired as home-health aides earning $30–40k less — all sits at the bottom under "Explore the full argument," labeled "Uncontested," below 45 expandable rows nobody will open. The page is front-loaded with apparatus and back-loaded with payoff, which is exactly backwards for a phone visitor. Fix: surface 3–5 "facts that would change the argument" as pull-quote cards between the positions and the cruxes.

  **4. The cruxes — the actual product — are unreadable as prose.**
  > "Firms facing improved AI capability respond primarily by reducing hiring and headcount growth rather than by expanding output or redesigning jobs to use the freed-up capacity. Empirical Contested Hidden assumption Thin evidence"

  This is the flagship feature, "computed from the argument structure, not editorial choice," and it reads like a database row: passive, nominalized, terminated by four unexplained tags ("Hidden assumption"? "Thin evidence"? — undefined jargon that implies the content is weak right as you're asking me to care about it). Fix: rewrite cruxes as questions ("When AI gets better, do firms hire less — or just produce more?") and define or kill the tags.

  **5. "Held by" attributions are name-drops that teach nothing.**
  > "Held by: Economic historians and diffusion-skeptic researchers (Narayanan & Kapoor, Autor's task-based framework, Acemoglu's modest-TFP estimate)..."

  Citations-as-decoration. A newcomer doesn't know who Autor is, and "Acemoglu's modest-TFP estimate" is insider shorthand. It performs rigor instead of delivering it, and eats two lines of the most valuable real estate on the page (the position cards). Fix: collapse to "Held by: economic historians, labor economists" and put names behind a tap. Save the authority for the evidence, where it earns it.

  **6. The trust apparatus swamps the content it protects.**
  > "Interest note: Upwork is a freelance platform whose revenue depends on freelance labor demand existing, giving it a commercial interest in an 'AI creates new freelance work' narrative."

  Every evidence item carries an interest note, a scope note, or both — often longer than the evidence itself, all rendered at the same visual weight. It's intellectually honest and experientially exhausting: per screen, maybe 40% of the words are meta-commentary about sources. The reader learns a lot about Argumend's scrupulousness and little about AI and jobs. Fix: a one-tap "⚠ conflict" badge with the note behind it. Same integrity, a fifth of the words.

  **7. The status taxonomy is incompressible and repeated to death.**
  "Empirical Contested" / "Predictive Contested" / "Empirical Broadly accepted" / "Status basis: ..." appears on all ~50 claims, and every "Status basis" paragraph restates the claim in meta-language ("This is the polluter-pays position in the corpus's own framing..."). "Corpus" — the word for the underlying research — appears constantly and means nothing to a visitor. It's the sound of the machine thinking out loud. Fix: icon for status, delete "Status basis" as a labeled field; fold the one genuinely useful sentence into the claim's context.

  **8. Zero emotional hook — nobody is on this page.**
  The debate is about whether millions of people lose their livelihoods, and the page contains no people. No "if you're a 23-year-old CS grad, this map is about you," no narrative entry point, no stakes framed in a life. The closest thing — the displaced-workers stat (earnings down 25% after ten years) — is line ~277, marked "Uncontested," under a collapsed section. A page about mass unemployment that never makes anyone feel anything will never be shared. Fix: open with a human-scale frame: "If you're 22 and graduating into tech, hiring is down 65% since 2019. Is that AI, interest rates, or offshoring? The entire debate turns on that question — here's the map."

  **9. "Explore the full argument" is a homework assignment, not an exploration.**
  > "Every claim in the map, with its evidence, objections, and status — nothing behind the curtain."

  "Nothing behind the curtain" is the tell: transparency as the selling point, which means the experience is *auditing*, not learning. ~45 flat, expandable claims with no grouping, no narrative order, no "start here." The ordering is arbitrary (a values claim about severance leads; the most dramatic evidence is last). Fix: group claims under the 4 positions they support ("What each side must believe"), or rank by surprise. Give the reader a path, not a pile.

  **10. The funnel teaches visitors to expect bureaucracy.**
  Homepage and Explore lead with "Balance 47 of 100, weight 69 of 100. Well-mapped, genuinely contested" on every card — two unexplained metrics and a tautological label ("contested... genuinely contested"), repeated ~30 times. "Balance 31/100 Against Balance of evidence For Weight of evidence" (homepage featured card) is genuinely broken as communication. So the flagship's first impression is pre-ruined: visitors arrive already trained that Argumend speaks in scores. Fix: kill the numeric chrome on cards; show the *question* and one provocative finding. "Balance/weight" belongs on the topic page, explained once.

  ## 3. The shareability question

  Right now there's nothing to quote. People share a *sentence* — a fact that re-frames something they thought they knew — and this page's every sentence is wrapped in qualifications and apparatus, so nothing is liftable. To earn "look at this," the page needs:

  - **3–5 standalone, screenshot-able insight cards** — one fact, one number, one line of context, no tags. The page has the material; it just refuses to present it.
  - **A verdict-shaped takeaway.** Not "here are 54 claims," but "here's what would settle this, and here's the one study both sides are fighting over." People share resolutions and live tensions, not maps.
  - **A provocation the reader can test against their life.** "Your job's AI exposure is mostly determined by whether you're a woman in a clerical role" sends people to their group chats.

  **The single most shareable thing buried in the page:** the Klarna reversal — the AI that "did the work of 700 agents," until the CEO admitted the cuts went too far and started rehiring humans — with the kicker that the 700 figure never meant 700 jobs eliminated. It's a complete story (hype → headline number → quiet reversal) that instantly teaches the page's core skill: headline AI-layoff numbers don't mean what you think. Second place: the entry-level paradox — 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs down 16%, while nearly half the tech hiring collapse predates ChatGPT. Either one, as a clean card at the top, is the difference between a page people forward and a page people admire.

  ## 4. Three copy rewrites

  **Worst #1** — the flagship's lead position card:
  > "AI is already causing measurable, AI-caused employment displacement concentrated in early-career and AI-exposed white-collar work, and this will compound into a broad U.S. unemployment crisis within 15 years."

  **Rewrite:** "The displacement has already started. Workers 22–25 in the most AI-exposed jobs are down 16% since ChatGPT — and the believers say that's the front edge of a crisis, not a blip."

  **Worst #2** — crux #1, the product's centerpiece:
  > "Firms facing improved AI capability respond primarily by reducing hiring and headcount growth rather than by expanding output or redesigning jobs to use the freed-up capacity. Empirical Contested Hidden assumption Thin evidence"

  **Rewrite:** "Crux 1: When AI makes a firm more productive, does it hire fewer people — or just sell more? Nobody has the firm-level data to tell. If it's 'fewer people,' the doomsayers win; if it's 'sell more,' history repeats and jobs come back."

  **Worst #3** — the topics-index card boilerplate, repeated 30+ times:
  > "Balance 47 of 100, weight 69 of 100. Well-mapped, genuinely contested"

  **Rewrite:** Replace entirely with the live tension: "Both sides agree sports betting is growing. They disagree about whether regulation contains the harm or launders it — and the evidence leans slightly against legalization."

  ## 5. What to cut

  - **The header stat line** ("4 positions · 54 claims · 50 pieces of evidence · 31 open disputes · 5 cruxes") — pure apparatus, first-impression poison. Delete.
  - **Every "Held by:" parenthetical name-drop** — collapse to a category ("labor economists"); names behind a tap.
  - **"Status basis" as a labeled field** on all ~50 claims — it's the methodology footer metastasized into every row. One useful sentence survives; the label and the boilerplate die.
  - **"Interest note" / "Scope note" at full text weight** — badge + tap-to-reveal. Same disclosure, 80% fewer words.
  - **The claim-type/status tag clusters** ("Empirical Contested Hidden assumption Thin evidence") — replace with one icon + color; define once in the methodology.
  - **"Balance X of 100, weight Y of 100" on every card** on homepage and Explore — invisible until the reader has a reason to care, i.e., inside the topic page.
  - **"Nothing behind the curtain" and all "corpus" language** — audit-speak; the reader wants an argument, not a filing cabinet.
  - **Roughly half of "Explore the full argument"** on first load — the 15–20 "Uncontested" background claims belong collapsed behind "Show the agreed-upon facts," so the contested core isn't diluted.
  - **The duplicated "Trending This Week / Topics" block** that appears identically on homepage and Explore — pick one home for it.
