## 1. First-screen verdict

Bounce. Four unreadable position labels, a database stats bar, no answer. Looks like academic homework, not an explanation. Zero reason to scroll on a phone.

## 2. Top 10 problems, ranked by shareability

**1. No payoff above the fold.**
- Offending: Title is `Will AI cause mass unemployment in the U.S. within the next 15 years?` followed immediately by `The positions` with no answer, summary, or takeaway.
- Why it fails: A busy stranger was promised "5 minutes to understand the disagreement." You give them a quiz with no answer key. If there's no insight in 5 seconds, they won't give 5 minutes.
- Fix: Put a 1-sentence TL;DR and a stakes line under the question. e.g. "Short answer: U-3 unemployment probably stays <5%, but a specific group is getting hammered on wages. The whole fight is about 5 questions." That's the thing people share.

**2. Position names are coded jargon.**
- Offending: `Displacement-now` / `Automation-panic redux` / `Wage-collapse, not unemployment` / `Institutional: who decides, and who pays`
- Why it fails: First-time reader has no idea what these mean, which one *they* are, or why they'd click. Sounds like chapters in a grad seminar, not sides in a real fight.
- Fix: Rename to human positions: "Yes, it's already starting" / "No, this panic happens every time" / "Not mass unemployment — mass pay cut" / "It's not what happens, it's who gets to decide"

**3. The gold (cruxes) is written like a dissertation abstract with tag soup.**
- Offending: `1 Firms facing improved AI capability respond primarily by reducing hiring and headcount growth rather than by expanding output or redesigning jobs to use the freed-up capacity. Empirical Contested Hidden assumption Thin evidence`
- Why it fails: The 5 cruxes are supposed to be the reason to share this page. Instead they are a single 27-word sentence + 4 bureaucrat tags. No human can parse or repeat it to a friend.
- Fix: Write each crux as a plain question headline. e.g. "When AI gets better, do bosses hire fewer people — or just make more stuff?" Put tags behind an icon. Human first, metadata second, hidden by default.

**4. The stats bar makes it feel like a database, not an experience.**
- Offending: `4 positions · 54 claims · 50 pieces of evidence · 31 open disputes · 5 cruxes`
- Why it fails: Immediately tells a phone reader "this is going to be work." Numbers about your internal model are not a benefit. It screams "unfinished internal tool."
- Fix: Delete entirely. Replace with social proof or human scope: "We read 360 sources, fights checked by 5 critics, so you don't have to."

**5. Homepage / Topics-index scores are incomprehensible gatekeepers.**
- Offending: `Balance 47 of 100, weight 69 of 100. Well-mapped, genuinely contested` and `5 computed cruxes · every claim, source, and assumption inspectable`
- Why it fails: New user has no idea what Balance or Weight mean (claim vs counterclaim? confidence? quality?). "Well-mapped, genuinely contested" is repeated on every card, so it means nothing. Featured description sells *process* ("inspectable"), not curiosity.
- Fix: Kill Balance/Weight from cards. Use plain English stakes: e.g. "Close fight, 360 sources" or "Most experts agree, but..." For featured, sell the *finding*, not the method: "The flagship: why most economists don't predict mass unemployment — but think 22-year-olds are screwed."

**6. "Held by:" lines are anonymous institution soup.**
- Offending: `Held by: Labor economists documenting entry-level hiring collapse (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, Census researchers), frontier-lab leaders forecasting rapid capability growth, and journalists covering AI-attributed layoffs.`
- Why it fails: A confused reader wants a face, a villain, a hero. You give a literature review citation. No trust anchor, no memorable character.
- Fix: Write as "Who says this: The Stanford team that found 16% drop for 22-25-year-olds in AI jobs, plus reporters tracking 100k+ AI-cited layoffs." Name the human, name the finding.

**7. "Explore the full argument" is an uncurated 45-claim dump that kills momentum.**
- Offending: `Explore the full argument — Every claim in the map, with its evidence, objections, and status — nothing behind the curtain.`
- Why it fails: After the 5 cruxes, you had them. Then you say "now read 45 more." The structure goes from "mostly comes down to" to "actually here is everything." No hierarchy, no story, infinite scroll.
- Fix: Hide it behind a tab / search. Default surface only 6-8 most surprising claims (Klarna 700 agents debunk, women 2.7x exposed, home-health aide $34.9k as #1 growth job). Label it "Rabbit holes for nerds" not "full argument."

**8. The methodology footer actively destroys trust.**
- Offending: `How this map was made: assembled by AI from a research corpus whose ~360 citations were liveness-checked, adversarially reviewed for balance in five directions, and validated against Argumend's argument model.`
- Why it fails: At the exact moment you need authority, you tell me a bot made it, using words like "liveness-checked" and "argument model." Reads like a disclaimer for hallucinations.
- Fix: Delete from flagship. Move to /about. Replace with credibility in human language at top: "360 sources linked, every claim has a source, show-us-we're-wrong button."

**9. "Why it's contested / What would settle it / Who has stakes here" is a rubric, not an explanation.**
- Offending: Repeated pattern: `Why it's contested This is an unstated mechanism assumption underlying the causal-attribution case...` / `What would settle it firm-level panels linking AI adoption to headcount...` / `Who has stakes here Strengthens Displacement-now Weakens Automation-panic redux`
- Why it fails: It feels like grading homework. The stakes line repeats the same two positions mechanically. A first-time reader doesn't want your ontology, they want "so what happens to me?"
- Fix: Rename to "Why people fight about it / What would prove it wrong / So what?" and write in second-person stakes once.

**10. No human stakes, no reason to care.**
- Offending: Buried at bottom: `Home health and personal care aide jobs are projected to be the single largest source of U.S. job growth from 2024 to 2034, adding about 740,000 jobs at a 2024 median wage of roughly $34,900.` and `Women are exposed to generative AI at roughly two to three times the rate of men`
- Why it fails: The most visceral, shareable implications (your $90k office job becomes a $34k care job; young grads hit first; women hit hardest) are formatted exactly like a footnote about token counts.
- Fix: Lead with people, not models. Add a "What this means for you" box near top: "If you're 22-25 in an office job, hiring down 16% since 2022. If you're in clerical work (91.9% women), you're in the #1 AI-exposed job family."

## 3. The shareability question

**What would it need to be shared with "look at this"?**
One visual that makes someone feel smarter in 10 seconds, plus one line they can steal at dinner. Right now there's nothing screenshot-able. You need a chart showing the divergence (e.g. "U-3 unemployment flat, entry-level AI-job hiring -16%, home-health $34.9k as biggest growth job"), a headline answer, and a single pull-quote designed to copy.

**The single most shareable thing buried right now:**
The reframe that it's not mass unemployment but wage-collapse into low-paid care work.

The most tweetable raw facts hidden in the dump:
- `Workers aged 22-25 in the most AI-exposed occupations experienced a 16% relative employment decline after controlling for firm-level shocks`
- `The "700 full-time agents" workload-equivalence figure for Klarna AI-assistant coverage does not establish that 700 employee positions were eliminated.`
- `Home health and personal care aides are projected to be the largest-growth U.S. occupation from 2024 to 2034: ... at a 2024 median wage of $34,900`
- `Female-dominated occupations are exposed to generative AI at 29% versus 16% for male-dominated occupations`

Any one of those, visualized, is more shareable than the entire current page.

## 4. Three copy rewrites

**Worst #1 - the stats bar:**
- Original: `4 positions · 54 claims · 50 pieces of evidence · 31 open disputes · 5 cruxes`
- Rewrite: `The short answer is no mass unemployment, yes brutal pay cuts for some. 5 fights actually decide it.`

**Worst #2 - crux as thesis sentence:**
- Original: `Firms facing improved AI capability respond primarily by reducing hiring and headcount growth rather than by expanding output or redesigning jobs to use the freed-up capacity. Empirical Contested Hidden assumption Thin evidence`
- Rewrite: `Do companies use AI to do more with fewer people, or to make more stuff with the same people? Nobody has tracked this properly yet — and the whole unemployment argument hinges on it.`

**Worst #3 - homepage label soup:**
- Original: `Balance 47 of 100, weight 69 of 100. Well-mapped, genuinely contested` and `5 computed cruxes · every claim, source, and assumption inspectable`
- Rewrite: `Close fight. 360 sources. Turns on whether entry-level jobs come back — or become $35k care jobs.`

## 5. What to cut

- **Delete the stats bar entirely** (`4 positions · 54 claims...`). It adds intimidation, zero value.
- **Delete Balance/Weight scores from all cards** on homepage and topics-index. If you keep scoring, hide it inside the map.
- **Hide the tag quartet** (`Empirical Contested Hidden assumption Thin evidence`) from crux headers. Move to a tooltip/details page.
- **Delete "The debate mostly comes down to — Unresolved questions with the most leverage — computed from the argument structure, not editorial choice."** Replace with "The whole argument turns on 5 questions."
- **Collapse or delete "Explore the full argument" default view**. Show 6 curated surprising claims, put the other 39 behind search/filter/hidden tab.
- **Delete the methodology footer from flagship**. Move to About. It reads like an AI-safety disclaimer and kills trust.
- **Delete repetitive "Who has stakes here — Strengthens X / Weakens Y"** blocks. One plain-English implication per crux max.
- **Delete repeated "Well-mapped, genuinely contested"** label on every topic card — it's visual noise that makes everything look the same.
