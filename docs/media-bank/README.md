### Verbatim excerpt

> "An ideal thing for me is like a one-hour conversation with one perspective
> with a person that's interlocating — that's actually asking good questions,
> offering some substantial pushback but letting [them] get the point out —
> and then another hour with the other side… It would be nice if there was a
> place where there was consensus on 'this is more or less what happened,'
> and then there was some editorialized: this is the perspective of people on
> the right, this is the perspective of people on the left."

### Why it matters

This is an independent articulation of Argumend's two-layer architecture:
a shared factual substrate ("consensus on more or less what happened") with
steelmanned perspectives layered on top, separated by camp rather than blended
into fake neutrality. The surrounding segment adds: debates decay into
humiliation rituals because positions are identity; the flagship question of
the day ("is this a genocide?") stacks definitional, empirical, and values
disputes into one unresolvable noise; and shared objects of disagreement are
social infrastructure against loneliness.
